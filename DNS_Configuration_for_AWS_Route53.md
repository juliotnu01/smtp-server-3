# DNS Configuration for Your Email Server in AWS Route 53

This guide provides a checklist and instructions for configuring the necessary DNS records in AWS Route 53 for your `docker-mailserver` setup on an EC2 instance.

## Prerequisites

Before you begin, ensure you have the following information ready:

1.  **Your Server's Elastic IP Address:** This is the static public IP address assigned to your EC2 instance (e.g., `198.51.100.123`). Replace all instances of `YOUR_SERVER_IP` with this IP.
2.  **Your Domain Name:** The domain you'll be using for email (e.g., `yourdomain.com`). Replace all instances of `yourdomain.com` with your actual domain.
3.  **Your Mail Server Hostname:** This is typically `mail.yourdomain.com`.
4.  **DKIM Public Key:** The public key generated by `docker-mailserver` (usually found in `./docker-data/dms/config/opendkim/keys/yourdomain.com/mail.txt` on your host, or as output by the `setup config dkim` command). It will look like `v=DKIM1; k=rsa; p=MIIBIjANBgk...`.

## DNS Record Checklist for AWS Route 53

Log in to your AWS Management Console and navigate to Route 53. Select your domain's "Hosted zone." For each record below, you will "Create record."

---

### 1. A Record (Address Record)

This record points your mail server's hostname (e.g., `mail.yourdomain.com`) to your server's Elastic IP address.

*   **Record name:** `mail` (If your domain is `yourdomain.com`, Route 53 will automatically append `.yourdomain.com`)
*   **Record type:** `A – Routes traffic to an IPv4 address and some AWS resources`
*   **Value:** `YOUR_SERVER_IP` (e.g., `198.51.100.123`)
*   **TTL (Seconds):** `300` (or your preferred TTL)

---

### 2. MX Record (Mail Exchange Record)

This record tells other mail servers where to deliver email for your domain.

*   **Record name:** Leave blank (or enter `@` if your DNS provider requires it for the root domain). This applies to `yourdomain.com`.
*   **Record type:** `MX – Specifies mail servers`
*   **Value:**
    ```
    10 mail.yourdomain.com
    ```
    (The `10` is the priority. `mail.yourdomain.com` is the mail server hostname you set up with the A record.)
*   **TTL (Seconds):** `3600` (or your preferred TTL)

---

### 3. SPF Record (Sender Policy Framework)

This TXT record specifies which IP addresses are authorized to send email for your domain, helping to prevent spoofing.

*   **Record name:** Leave blank (or enter `@` if your DNS provider requires it for the root domain). This applies to `yourdomain.com`.
*   **Record type:** `TXT – Used to verify email senders and for application-specific values`
*   **Value:**
    ```
    "v=spf1 ip4:YOUR_SERVER_IP -all"
    ```
    (e.g., `"v=spf1 ip4:198.51.100.123 -all"`)
    *   **Note on `Value`:** The value **must be enclosed in double quotes** in the Route 53 TXT record value field.
    *   `-all`: Mails from servers not listed are explicitly unauthorized.
    *   `~all` (soft fail): Mails from unlisted servers are marked as suspicious but may still be accepted. You can start with `~all` for initial testing if you are concerned about deliverability, then move to `-all`.

*   **TTL (Seconds):** `3600` (or your preferred TTL)

---

### 4. DKIM Record (DomainKeys Identified Mail)

This TXT record adds a digital signature to outgoing emails, allowing receiving servers to verify that the email was sent by an authorized server and hasn't been tampered with.

*   **Record name:** `mail._domainkey` (If your domain is `yourdomain.com`, Route 53 will append `.yourdomain.com`. The `mail` part is the DKIM selector, defined by `docker-mailserver` by default. If you used a different selector, adjust accordingly.)
*   **Record type:** `TXT – Used to verify email senders and for application-specific values`
*   **Value:**
    ```
    "v=DKIM1; k=rsa; p=YOUR_FULL_DKIM_PUBLIC_KEY_STRING"
    ```
    (e.g., `"v=DKIM1; k=rsa; p=MIIBIjANBgkqhkiG9w0BAQEFAAOCAQ8AMIIBCgKCAQEAyourPublicKeyString..."`)
    *   **Note on `Value`:**
        *   The entire string **must be enclosed in double quotes**.
        *   Copy the DKIM key exactly as provided by `docker-mailserver` from the `mail.txt` file (e.g., from `./docker-data/dms/config/opendkim/keys/yourdomain.com/mail.txt`).
        *   Route 53 TXT records have a limit of 255 characters per string. If your DKIM key is longer (e.g., 2048-bit keys), you need to split it into multiple quoted strings within the same TXT record value. Route 53 handles this automatically if you paste the long key into the console value field – it will visually show it split into "value chunks".
        Example for a long key: `"v=DKIM1; k=rsa; p=stringpart1..." "stringpart2..."`

*   **TTL (Seconds):** `3600` (or your preferred TTL)

---

### 5. DMARC Record (Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting & Conformance)

This TXT record tells receiving mail servers how to handle emails that fail SPF and/or DKIM checks, and provides reporting addresses.

*   **Record name:** `_dmarc` (If your domain is `yourdomain.com`, Route 53 will append `.yourdomain.com`)
*   **Record type:** `TXT – Used to verify email senders and for application-specific values`
*   **Value:**
    ```
    "v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc-reports@yourdomain.com; ruf=mailto:dmarc-forensic@yourdomain.com; fo=1"
    ```
    *   **Note on `Value`:** The value **must be enclosed in double quotes**.
    *   `p=none`: This is a monitoring policy. Start with `none` to receive reports without affecting email delivery. You can later change to `p=quarantine` or `p=reject` once you are confident your setup is correct.
    *   `rua=mailto:dmarc-reports@yourdomain.com`: Aggregate reports will be sent here.
    *   `ruf=mailto:dmarc-forensic@yourdomain.com`: Forensic (failure) reports will be sent here.
    *   You should create these email addresses (e.g., `dmarc-reports@yourdomain.com`, `dmarc-forensic@yourdomain.com`) on your mail server or use an alias/forwarding rule.

*   **TTL (Seconds):** `3600` (or your preferred TTL)

---

## PTR Record (Reverse DNS) Configuration

The PTR record (also known as Reverse DNS or rDNS) maps your server's IP address back to its designated hostname (e.g., `YOUR_SERVER_IP` maps to `mail.yourdomain.com`). This is critical for email deliverability, as many mail servers reject emails from IPs without valid PTR records.

**IMPORTANT:** You **cannot** configure PTR records directly within your Route 53 hosted zone like the records above.

**How to configure PTR records for an AWS Elastic IP:**

1.  **AWS Responsibility:** AWS manages the reverse DNS zone for its IP address ranges.
2.  **Request Process:** You need to request AWS to create or update the PTR record for your Elastic IP address.
3.  **Steps (General Guidance - AWS Console may change):**
    *   Go to the EC2 Dashboard in the AWS Management Console.
    *   Under "Network & Security," find "Elastic IPs."
    *   Select your Elastic IP address.
    *   Look for an option like "Actions" -> "Update reverse DNS" or similar. If this option is not directly visible, AWS documentation states you might need to fill out a specific request form.
    *   You will need to provide the Elastic IP and the hostname it should point to (e.g., `mail.yourdomain.com`).
    *   AWS has specific requirements for the hostname, such as it must have a corresponding A record pointing back to the IP.
4.  **AWS Documentation:** Search the AWS documentation for "Reverse DNS for Amazon EC2" or "Configuring reverse DNS for an Elastic IP address" for the most current instructions. Here's a general link to AWS EC2 documentation that often covers this: [AWS EC2 User Guide](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AWSEC2/latest/UserGuide/elastic-ip-addresses-eip.html#Using_Elastic_Addressing_Reverse_DNS) (You'll need to navigate or search within for "reverse DNS").
5.  **Processing Time:** It might take some time for AWS to process the request and for the PTR record to propagate.

---

## DNS Propagation and Verification

DNS changes are not instantaneous. They can take anywhere from a few minutes to 48 hours to fully propagate across the internet, although Route 53 changes are often faster.

**Recommended Verification Tools:**

1.  **MXToolbox (Web-based):**
    *   Go to [mxtoolbox.com](https://mxtoolbox.com/).
    *   You can use their tools to look up MX, SPF, DKIM, DMARC, A, and PTR records for your domain and IP. This is very helpful for diagnosing issues.

2.  **`dig` (Command-Line Interface):**
    If you have access to a Linux or macOS terminal, `dig` is a powerful tool. Replace `yourdomain.com` with your actual domain and `mail.yourdomain.com` with your mail server's hostname.

    *   **Check A Record for mail host:**
        ```bash
        dig mail.yourdomain.com A +short
        ```
        (Should return `YOUR_SERVER_IP`)

    *   **Check MX Record:**
        ```bash
        dig yourdomain.com MX +short
        ```
        (Should return something like `10 mail.yourdomain.com`)

    *   **Check SPF Record (TXT):**
        ```bash
        dig yourdomain.com TXT +short
        ```
        (Should show your SPF string, e.g., `"v=spf1 ip4:YOUR_SERVER_IP -all"`)

    *   **Check DKIM Record (TXT):**
        ```bash
        dig mail._domainkey.yourdomain.com TXT +short
        ```
        (Should show your DKIM string, e.g., `"v=DKIM1; k=rsa; p=..."`. May be split into multiple quoted parts if long.)

    *   **Check DMARC Record (TXT):**
        ```bash
        dig _dmarc.yourdomain.com TXT +short
        ```
        (Should show your DMARC string, e.g., `"v=DMARC1; p=none; ..."`)

    *   **Check PTR Record (Reverse DNS Lookup):**
        Replace `YOUR_SERVER_IP` with your actual Elastic IP.
        ```bash
        dig -x YOUR_SERVER_IP +short
        ```
        (Should return `mail.yourdomain.com` after AWS has processed your rDNS request.)

---

Once these records are correctly set up and have propagated, your mail server will have a much better chance of delivering emails successfully and not being flagged as spam. Regularly check your DMARC reports and use tools like mail-tester.com to assess your email sending reputation.
